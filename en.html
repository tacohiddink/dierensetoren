The Dierense Toren

A neo-Gothic church tower from 1925 in the village of Dieren, between Arnhem and Zutphen on the river IJssel. The church is gone, but the tower was saved by the village and is now a protected monument.

Visit

Climb the tower on Heritage Day

During the Dutch Heritage Days (Open Monumentendag), the tower opens to the public. Climb up to the bell loft and enjoy the view over Dieren and the IJssel landscape through the sound openings.

Heritage Day 2027: Saturday 11 and Sunday 12 September. Opening times are announced on the Dutch activities page and on openmonumentendag.nl.

Every Saturday at 6 pm its three bells ring out over the village.

Getting there

  • Address: Hogestraat 12, 6953 AS Dieren
  • By train: about a 5-minute walk from Dieren station, through the Carolinapark.
  • By car or bike: the tower has its own car park, on the site of the former church. The car park is wheelchair accessible.

History

A tower the village refused to lose

1895
The Catholic church of Our Lady of the Assumption opens on the Hogestraat, still without a tower.
1924–1925
A bequest from Frits Reekers pays for the tower, designed by architect Wolter te Riele. It is consecrated on 17 May 1925.
1943 · 1948
The bells are taken by the German occupiers. In 1948 three new bells are hung, cast by Jos. Zimmer & Zonen in Amsterdam.
1976
The church is demolished. After protests by the residents and the action group Comité K12, the tower is spared.
1985
The foundation wins the Dutch Conservation Award for giving the tower a new use.
1987
Restored and reopened as a meeting place with offices and a viewpoint.
2025
The tower turns 100. The centenary festival pays for new lighting; the tower is lit every evening again.

Today the tower is a municipal monument of Rheden (no. G-1005186200), cared for by volunteers. Read the full story in Dutch on the history page.

Help keep the tower standing
